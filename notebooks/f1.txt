\subsection*{Primary Metric: Alert Intensity and Breach Assessment}

Alert Intensity is selected as the primary monitoring metric because it normalizes ECM alert volumes to the monitored population, making the measure independent of customer growth, portfolio expansion, and workflow capacity changes. This provides the most stable and interpretable indicator of monitoring pressure and enables like-for-like comparison across intelligence-flagged (I) and non-intelligence (NI) cohorts.

\begin{equation}
\text{Alert Intensity}_m^{I} = \frac{A_m^{I}}{N_m},
\qquad
\text{Alert Intensity}_m^{NI} = \frac{A_m^{NI}}{N_m}
\end{equation}

Two complementary breach methodologies are applied to the monthly Alert Intensity series for each cohort.

\paragraph{1. Standard Deviation Approach}

Let $\mu^{I}$ and $\sigma^{I}$ denote the historical mean and standard deviation of $\text{Alert Intensity}^{I}$; similarly for NI.

\begin{equation}
\text{Breach}_{m}^{I} =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{I} > \mu^{I} + k\sigma^{I} \\
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{I} < \mu^{I} - k\sigma^{I} \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\text{Breach}_{m}^{NI} =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{NI} > \mu^{NI} + k\sigma^{NI} \\
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{NI} < \mu^{NI} - k\sigma^{NI} \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{2. Robust Median–MAD Approach}

Let $\tilde{A}^{I}$ be the median of $\text{Alert Intensity}^{I}$ and  
$MAD^{I} = \text{median}\left(|\text{Alert Intensity}^{I}_m - \tilde{A}^{I}|\right)$; similarly for NI.

\begin{equation}
\text{Breach}_{m}^{I} =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{I} > \tilde{A}^{I} + k \cdot MAD^{I} \\
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{I} < \tilde{A}^{I} - k \cdot MAD^{I} \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\text{Breach}_{m}^{NI} =
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{NI} > \tilde{A}^{NI} + k \cdot MAD^{NI} \\
1, & \text{if } \text{Alert Intensity}_m^{NI} < \tilde{A}^{NI} - k \cdot MAD^{NI} \\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

The standard deviation method provides sensitivity to sustained structural shifts, while the MAD-based method offers robustness to outliers and intelligence-driven spikes, ensuring balanced and stable breach detection for both I and NI segments.
